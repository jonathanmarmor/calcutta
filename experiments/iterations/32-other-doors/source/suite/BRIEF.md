# Other Doors — public album brief

Original request (2026-10-21): “Let's make another album now. Make it about an hour long. Make lots of variety both between the pieces and with them. Vary the length and compositional form. Some could be tiny. Some could be major works. Have some be very unexpected departures, potentially on many dimensions. You could invent an entirely new way of generating pieces based on the same tuning system. Go wild. But overall it should stay true the iterations we've been working on.”

Public composition specification:
- Twelve new pieces with uneven lengths: 0:52, 3:53, 6:58, 1:21, 9:35, 3:15, 0:41, 6:10, 2:09, 10:48, 2:56, 11:00. Total music 59:38 plus eleven two-second gaps yields 60:00. Titles and form descriptions live in compose_doors.TRACKS.
- Fresh deterministic seeds and material. Vary form, pace, density, protagonists and orchestration inside pieces. Change substantial texture/ensemble every 25 seconds at most; variable scene lengths and section tempo plateaus rather than identical four-bar blocks.
- Use the established 97 dry fixed-frequency sample-derived timbre models, varying instrument inventories and ensembles per piece. Preserve synthetic, acoustic, electric and blended colors. No vibrato, chorus, detune, reverb, delay, click or master ending fade. Keep prompt attack/release and gentle dynamics.
- Distinct main engines: answering choirs; voice-leading/common-tone graph of triads and sevenths; original construction-tree endpoint clocks; simultaneous phrase clocks with rational tempo relationships; a cue network in which phrase events generate later instrumental entries; hocket; perceptible chord-stack sequences; additive/subtractive and registral processes. These are implemented algorithms, not just titles attached to reseeded scores.
- Hard amplitude gates are confined to track 6, with ungated contrast within that track. Different rates, phases and duties produce the interlocks. One-millisecond edge smoothing prevents incidental sample discontinuities; gates never alter pitch.
- Eleven pieces traverse all forty original fields through advancing contiguous harmonic windows, opening on field index 1 (2:3), visiting index 0 (1:2) only briefly afterward, and devoting their last 40% to field 39. The 41-second track 7 is a deliberate exception: it remains entirely in field 39, changing register and instrumentation inside that field.
- All written pitches are exact original field members. Passing tones are permitted by prior practice but are not needed on this album. No transposed octave substitutes, added harmony or held-note retuning. Instruments can move among actual adjacent field pitches. Distinguish MIDI note pitch from the harmonic/inharmonic partial spectra that constitute instrumental timbre.
- New piece-specific material and dry individual exits, rather than a shared loud two-short/one-long chord gesture or fading master. Layered textures predominate in larger pieces, with true sparse exceptions and deliberately different miniatures.
- Save all source, note/voice decisions, harmonic windows, phrase clocks, relay cues, model coefficients, gate parameters, beat grids, MIDI, render audits, validation, public instructions and a chronological repo entry. Preserve the continuous album, individual listening files and a guide. Do not include hidden reasoning.

Implementation limits and provenance:
- The 97 models are inherited from Wide Palette/Prism Switchyard, not new live plugin instances or untouched acoustic recordings. The sampled source family is GeneralUser GS; eight earlier analog/FM captures provide synthetic colors. Their fixed-frequency reconstruction deliberately discards pitch modulation. Related presets count as different source sounds.
- Graph choices use approximate interval matching and voice-leading cost; a single MIDI pitch remains an exact original field pitch even when a chord subset is selected approximately by its internal interval shape.
- MIDI preserves pitches, timing and program hints; amplitude-gate DSP and the custom timbre reconstruction are rendered by the supplied audio code, not encoded in the MIDI.
