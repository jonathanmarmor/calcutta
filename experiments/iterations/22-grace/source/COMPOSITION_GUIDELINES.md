# Continuing Calcutta composition preferences

- Create entirely new musical material and a new form for every generation. Reuse useful techniques and sounds, not prior motifs, note patterns, phrases, or the repeated short-short-long dense-chord gesture.
- Default pitch rule: structural and melodic notes use exact original endpoints, without octave transposition. Grace and Grain alone has explicit permission for outside-field passing tones and ornaments; that exception expires with that generation.
- Prefer no held-note retuning across harmonic changes: end with a breath, rearticulate after a gap, or sustain an unchanged exact common pitch.
- The 40 original pitch collections are the source. Harmonic order/rhythm may be expressive, with local returns and subsets; somewhere expose every original collection in full. Do not start on the 1:2 collection or make it the opening focus.
- Instruments may move among actual pitches and form temporary choirs. Preserve construction positions as pitch metadata, rather than requiring each instrument to stay fixed to one position.
- Explore characteristic triads, seventh extensions, unusual steps, and voice-leading within fields as well as between fields.
- No click unless asked. Keep sounds dry: no delay, reverb, or chorus. Prefer no vibrato or detuned/unison sounds. Immediate gated releases with short smoothing are appropriate.
- Rubato, section tempo changes, intentional anticipation/delay, tuplets, rhythmic suspensions, and many similar independent pulses are now welcome. These should have expressive shape rather than arbitrary jitter.
- Research historical artist configurations with primary interviews where possible; separate documented details, inferred programming recipes, and modern reconstructions.
