# Slow Apertures — public generation instructions

User request: “Can you make a movement that is mostly sustaining tones covering the full range of these harmonies with very long fade ins and fade outs of different timbres. You can also make a texture on a note or chord that enters and exists very very gradually either through fading or gradually building the texture. Textures can include trills or dense arpeggiations whatever else you come up with”

Implementation plan:
- One 14-minute movement, Slow Apertures. The main material is 688 individually enveloped sustained instrumental strands across the original four-octave-plus register. Use the established fixed-frequency timbre models, reconstructed from their steady spectra rather than attack/pluck envelopes.
- Individual long-tone entrances are approximately 18–49 seconds and exits 22–61 seconds, with sin-squared ramps and independent onset/duration/timbre. No sudden tutti entrances. Timbral waves overlap and near-unisons receive different source colors.
- All forty original fields appear, starting on index 1 (2:3), with only a quieter early passing visit to index 0 (1:2). New field launches are twelve seconds apart, while old tones retain their own fixed source pitches and fade across those launches. Deliberate overlapping source fields create actual crossfades; no tone bends or retunes to the new field. This supersedes the previous abrupt note-off/harmonic-boundary policy for this movement.
- The final field begins emerging around 7:55–8:00. Only field 39 remains after 9:18, so the last third is entirely that field. Its internal timbral waves continue changing.
- Eight slowly emerging event clouds: neighboring-pitch trills, widening arpeggiations, triad/seventh mist and sparse-to-dense lattices. Each cloud has its own roughly two-minute amplitude envelope and a separate density/probability ramp. Individual particles are soft and secondary to the sustained layer. Every pitched event uses a recorded original source-field pitch; no octave substitutes or passing tones are added.
- Keep the entire register present through most of the body, with gentler upper-register gains. Use fixed stereo placement, no reverb, delay, chorus, detune, pitch LFO, vocals or click. End by letting individual strands/clouds withdraw at different times. No master ending fade is used; the requested fades are explicit note and texture envelopes.
- Preserve exact score/source-field assignments, note/cloud envelopes, event probabilities in deterministic code, steady timbre models/provenance/license, MIDI with expression curves, audio audit and regeneration/coverage validation. Public instructions are retained; hidden reasoning is not included.

The earlier instruction against slow attacks is intentionally superseded by this request for very long fade-ins and fade-outs. “Dry” here still means no room/reverb/delay, not an immediate amplitude envelope. Acoustic/electric and analog/FM colors are the inherited sample-derived resynthesized models, not newly installed plugins or untouched recordings.
