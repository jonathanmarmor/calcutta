# Calcutta: Four Volumes — public generation specification

User request (2026-10-21): “Might as well make another. Try out different approaches to ending the pieces, and don't repeat the same approach. You can experiment with DSP and effects in one or two of the pieces. In one or two pieces you can either subtly diverge from the tuning system or drastically diverge. It might even provide some contrast to have a 12 ET piece somewhere thrown in. Also experiment with some more traditional musical textures here and there, or focused in one piece. You can also explore synthesizing singing voice in one or two pieces. Let's make this a slightly different format than an album, with perhaps 3-5 large volumes of 20-80 minutes each. Maybe one piece should go hard on trills, or another on glissandi.”

Public implementation plan:
- Four continuous volumes: 24:00 Instruments of Return, 28:00 The Singing Circuit, 32:00 Fault Lines, 36:00 Long Weather. Sixteen movements total 119:36 of music, plus twelve two-second internal gaps = 120:00. Each volume has four movement chapters. plan.py records titles, lengths, movement descriptions and distinct ending procedures.
- Keep the established dry sampled-source/analog/FM palette, prompt attacks, short releases, no vibrato or chorus, and no click. Use fresh seeds and note material. New traditional-texture procedures include chorale, chaconne/ground bass, imitative invention and solo/accompaniment concertino, alongside cue networks, multi-tempo lines, hocket, chord-subset walks and changing registers/ensembles.
- Keep changing substantial texture or ensemble within 30 seconds, usually sooner. Each movement has its own recurring procedural palette, and a specifically composed ending rather than the inherited default fragment or master fade.
- Fourteen movements have only exact original Calcutta field pitches. They preserve advancing adjacent harmonic neighborhoods and final-field residence for the last 40%. Movement 9, An Ordinary Sky, is a genuinely tonal 12-ET departure in C, with traditional diatonic materials and an explicit G7-to-C cadence. Its inherited field labels are scheduling markers rather than actual Calcutta harmonies. Movement 10, Continuous Ground, uses continuous smooth pitch trajectories between original field pitches, with fixed-pitch anchors and a final convergence. These are the only two written-pitch departures.
- Movement 7, The Trill Orchard, emphasizes alternating discrete field pitches, with varying rates, supporting sustained lines and a final deceleration to the lower neighbor. Trills are articulated notes, not a pitch LFO. Movement 10 is the concentrated glissando study; its endpoint slides are integrated continuously in the renderer and sampled into MIDI pitch-bend curves.
- Movement 5, Mouths of Light, and movement 8, A Sentence Interrupted, add original wordless singing-like voices. Synthesis uses harmonic glottal excitation shaped by vowel formants, brief consonant/breath noise, and fixed fundamental frequency; no voice cloning, recordings, external singer service or vibrato. The intended result is an audible synthetic vowel ensemble, not a claim of realistic intelligible human singing.
- Effects are restricted to movement 6, Echoes Have Their Own Meter (finite multi-tap networks), and movement 12, The Reversible Room (reversed grains and envelope). Ordinary note gates and instrumental/vocal synthesis do not count as post effects. Keep the rest dry. Effects stay inside allocated note/harmonic spans; no old harmony is sustained/retuned across a change. The echo ending explicitly stops excitation before the finite repeat tail ends.
- Endings: answered question/fifth; disappearing pulse with two isolated colors; ground completed under unresolved upper line; successive players leave; vowel closes to hum; echo-only continuation; trill slows/settles; interrupted new vocal sentence; authentic 12-ET cadence; glissandi meet in unison; unanswered call/silence; reverse-resonance breath; opening phrase reorchestrated; onsets spread in time without a volume fade; soloist leaves and ensemble replies; a final phrase folds back in event-level retrograde. No shared automatic master fade. Event retrograde in movement 16 is compositional ordering, not audio reversal DSP.
- Freeze all code, original pitch input, model coefficients/provenance/license, public instructions, note and voice decisions, phrase/cue data, endpoints and continuous curves, effect settings, endings, MIDI, tempo maps, validation and regeneration checks. Keep the chronological repo archive current.

The 97 inherited instrumental source models are sample-derived fixed-frequency reconstructions from earlier GeneralUser GS and analog/FM captures, not newly installed plugins or untouched acoustic recordings. Four new formant-voice identities are synthesized in code. Public generation instructions and implementation decisions are retained; hidden reasoning is not included.
